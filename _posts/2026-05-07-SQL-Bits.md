# Background
I have heard a lot about SQL bits from collogues who have been involved in various ways over the years so it had a lot to live up to. I decided to brave the 3:30 drive to Newport for the Free Saturday this year to join a few hundred geeks (8 of which I work with).

# Sessions
There was too much to choose from so I went with focusing on areas that I am least familiar with.

## AI
I went to a two part session with Scott Bell and Mihaly Kavasl called "Building context, not vibes practical AI augmented Data Engineering" which was a deep dive into current capabilities and trends in the world of AI coding. It was good to see that a lot of what I had been doing was on the right lines but was also great to have more of a framework and the right language to make things more reliable, repeatable and predictable.

The second AI focused session I attended was by Kevin Arnold called "Next-Level Bi Development: AI-Driven Semantic Models in Power BI". This session was really interesting because I had used the Fabric MCP server for the first time earlier that week and made the mistake of trying to get it to do Power BI related stuff without realising there was a Power BI specific MCP which would have been better suited. The focus of the workshop was on practical "100 Level" usage of the Power BI MCP plugin for VS Code.

## Engineering
The UTF specific session by Oskari Heikkinen called "User Data Functions: Development, Testing and Integration" had some of the biggest lightbulb moments for me. The content focused on Transdactal Workflows which I had seen before but it was the demo of the Local Development in VS code and the test scripts he had created for the UTF that sparked a key takeaway for me.

## Security
The other two part session I attended was "OneLake Security - Centralized Data Security for Microsoft Fabric" With Gabi Münster, Thierry Houy and Edgar Cotte from Microsoft. As they said Integrated security has been a big promise of Fabric since launch and is very close to being realised and moved into GA. The demo was really interesting and brings new tools and new changes to ways of working in Fabric.

**Update** - I waited to long between writing this and finishing and One Lake Security is now GA at time of publishing 2-3 weeks later

## Soft Skills
The final session I attended was by my colleague Robert French who in his Kilt brought his usual level of passion to a fantastic session covering lessons learnt and approaches that work for working with colleagues and customers. Robert's aim was for one person to take away one thing from his talk and his message about being honest and taking ownership of mistakes is the key thing that mixed with the testing from Oskari's session into a testing framework that I started working on the next day.

# Themes and Takeaways
## AI Is Everywhere
Every session I attended included reference or core demonstrations of AI - that is probably unsurprising for the AI specific sessions but speaking to people who attended other sessions I doubt there were any talks where this was not a theme. A couple of times people referenced the concern "AI is going to take my job" which is something I have never subscribed to.

The analogy I use for AI is the impact of email in workplaces. The change there was more gradual and I entered the workforce while the last gasps of printing memos and postal communication (which by then was just sales and subscriptions). The change was gradual but the main casualties of email were in post room jobs in larger organisations, every one else (including the postal system) pivoted and learnt to adapt to how technology was accelerating work. AI has not been gradual and has much wider scope than something like Email but I think our work will adapt to the technology and the pace of what we can do will increase - there will be plenty of disrupted areas (particularly if they can nail driverless cars) and for creative careers but for us data geeks we get to be the tools of the robot overloads not the victims.

## Testing is not optional
During Robert's talk I wrote down the phrase "Popodies Nerfect" and it is true that all pipelines will eventually fail so it is important to set expectations with clients and to own the mistakes that you will make, but, you don't want to have to defend the indefensible.

I will apologise but I want to be able to evidence and show that best efforts were taken to avoid the issue that I am saying sorry for. This is my biggest takeaway of the day - what can we do to ensure that we can demonstrate that every effort has been taken to test the stuff we build and how can we make testing something that is part of what we build?

# Conclusion
Thank you to everyone involved in [SQL Bits](https://sqlbits.com/) I really enjoyed the day and the talks that I attended and was well worth the drive👍
